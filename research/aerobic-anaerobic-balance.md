# aerobic vs anaerobic balance: what the research says (oct 2026)

written for a rider at ftp ~290 w, 8–10 h/week, with a punchy profile (5-min power well above 120% of ftp), aiming for 330 w in about six months.

## verdict

train **pyramidal now, more polarized from february**. keep about 75–80% of time easy, 12–18% at sub-threshold/threshold, and 5–8% above threshold. your limiter is **threshold as a fraction of your top end**, not your anaerobic capacity, so spend the hard work on sub-threshold volume and 30/15 short intervals. keep anaerobic work as small, regular doses that add variety without wrecking the key sessions.

## what the evidence says

1. **intensity distribution: no single model wins.**
   - **oliveira et al. 2024:** 17 studies, n = 437. polarized gave a small vo2peak edge (smd 0.24) but **no difference in time-trial performance**. the vo2peak edge appeared mainly in highly trained athletes and in blocks under 12 weeks.
   - **rosenblat et al. 2025:** network meta-analysis of individual participant data, 13 studies, n = 348. no overall winner between polarized and pyramidal. exploratory: recreational athletes may do better on pyramidal, competitive athletes on polarized.
   - **filipas et al. 2022:** pyramidal base then polarized before competition gave the largest gains (runners).
2. **pros train pyramidal.**
   - **worldtour men, 2026 study:** 70% low, 25% moderate, 4% high intensity (by power) in pre-season, about 19 h/week.
   - **worldtour season analysis, 2025:** distribution stays pyramidal all season; the high-intensity share rises towards racing.
3. **zone 2 is not magic.** storoschuk, gibala, gurd et al. 2025: for trained people, fitness gains come from intensities above z2. z2 is the volume that lets you absorb the hard work and builds durability; it isn't the stimulus itself. so making z2 more interesting (cadence work, terrain, sprinkles) costs nothing.
4. **short intervals beat long intervals for threshold power.**
   - **rønnestad 2015:** 30/15s (3 × 13 × 30 s / 15 s) vs 4 × 5 min. over 10 weeks the 30/15 group gained more: 40-min power +12% vs +4%, power at 4 mmol +12% vs +5%.
   - **rønnestad 2020, elite riders:** 20-min power +4.7% vs −1.4% in 3 weeks.
5. **interval type matters less than doing it consistently.** norte et al. 2023, meta-analysis of trained cyclists: 2–3 interval sessions per week on a low-intensity base. no clear winner between sprint, short and long intervals; short intervals were the most consistent.
6. **sprint intervals work but are a small lever for trained riders.**
   - **de poli et al. 2026:** 4–6 × 30 s all-out raised power at the respiratory compensation point (roughly threshold) by 11% in recreationally active men.
   - **norte 2023:** sprint intervals were not significantly better than continuous training in trained cyclists.
   - use them as a maintenance or variety dose, not a block focus.
7. **durability is the fourth dimension.**
   - riders who hold power late in rides beat riders with the same fresh numbers (leo 2021, van erp 2021, mateo-march 2022).
   - **spragg 2022:** linked better durability to more low-intensity volume.
   - **norte et al. 2026:** fuelling at 120 g/h cut the drop in critical power after 3 h (277 → 266 w, vs 257 w at 60 g/h and 236 w with water only).
8. **heavy strength training helps.**
   - **rønnestad & mujika 2014, review:** 4–10 rm, 2–3 sets, 2 sessions a week for about 12 weeks, then 1 session a week to maintain. it improves peak power and late-ride power: +7% in a 5-min effort after 3 h (rønnestad 2011).
   - endurance riders are best served by heavy, multi-joint leg work.
9. **the norwegian method (double threshold, lactate 2–3 mmol/l) is a high-volume elite tool.** broken sub-threshold intervals (e.g., 4–5 × 8–10 min at about 88–93% ftp) are the transferable part. double days aren't realistic at 8–10 h/week.

## what this means in practice

| week element | prescription | why |
|---|---|---|
| key 1 (tue) | sub-threshold/threshold: 4 × 8 → 3 × 12 → 2 × 20 at 88–95% (255–275 w) | builds threshold as a fraction of top end, your limiter |
| key 2 (thu) | 30/15s: 3 × 13 × 30 s at 120–125% (345–360 w), 15 s easy | best evidence for raising 20-min power and power at 4 mmol |
| z2 days | rotating formats + 6–8 × 10-s sprinkles once a week | variety; keeps sprint snap; z2 is the base, not the stimulus |
| sun long ride | 2–3 h z2, last 30–40 min with 2 × 10 min at 88–90% from december | durability: quality work on tired legs. fuel 90–120 g/h |
| anaerobic dose | every 2nd week: 4–6 × 20–30 s hard at the end of a z2 ride (never the day before a key session) | keeps your top end; low cost |
| strength | nov–feb: 2 × week heavy legs (4–8 rm: squat/leg press, rdl, split squat). mar–apr: 1 × week | late-ride power, durability |
| distribution target | ~78/16/6 (low/moderate/high, by time) until feb; ~80/10/10 feb–apr | pyramidal base → polarized before the peak (filipas) |
